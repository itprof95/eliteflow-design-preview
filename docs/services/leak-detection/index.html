WHAT WE CAN HELP WITH

Find the source before deciding on the repair

Water does not always appear directly beneath a leaking connection. It can travel along plumbing or surfaces before becoming visible. The first wet spot is a useful clue, but it does not identify the failed part by itself. Describe whether the moisture appears continuously or after using a particular fixture.

Leak assessment can involve accessible connections, the affected area and the plumbing layout. The methods and access needed depend on the property. Confirm any specialist investigation available and whether opening a wall, ceiling or floor may be necessary. Once the source is understood, the next step may be fixture repair, a connection repair or pipe replacement.

Clues that deserve a closer look

  • A drip returns after an area is wiped dry.
  • Dampness appears beneath a sink or near a plumbing route.
  • A stain changes or reappears after fixture use.
  • Water usage changes without an obvious explanation.

YOUR QUESTIONS, ANSWERED

Leak detection questions

Does a stain always mean a plumbing leak?

No. A stain alone does not establish its source. Explain when it appears and what nearby fixtures are doing so the issue can be assessed rather than assumed.

Will a wall or floor need to be opened?

It depends on the leak's location and available access. Ask about investigation options and any proposed opening before approving work. A damage-free detection guarantee is not offered here.

Is finding a leak the same as repairing it?

No. Detection establishes the source; repair addresses the failed plumbing. Eliteflow also lists fixture and pipe repairs. The repair and any restoration should be discussed separately as needed.

Should I use the form if water is spreading?

Call (571) 682-5754 for an urgent issue. A standard online request is suitable for a stable concern and does not guarantee immediate assistance.
